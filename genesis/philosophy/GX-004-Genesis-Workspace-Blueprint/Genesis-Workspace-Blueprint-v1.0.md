# Genesis Workspace Blueprint v1.0

Project: Genesis Enterprise Operating System  
Studio: Genesis Experience Studio  
Sprint: GX-004  
Artifact: Genesis Workspace Blueprint  
Version: v1.0  
Status: FOUNDATIONAL EXPERIENCE TEMPLATE

## Mission
Create the canonical workspace blueprint that every Genesis workspace shall inherit.

This blueprint defines repeatable experience architecture for all future Genesis workspaces.

This is not an implementation specification.

This is not an engineering standard.

This is not a constitutional artifact.

This is a reusable product experience blueprint.

## Objective
Establish a universal blueprint so every Genesis workspace feels like part of one Enterprise Operating System while still specializing around its own mission.

## Core Principle
Every workspace answers one primary business question better than any competing product.

Each workspace should immediately reduce uncertainty for its intended audience.

## 1. Workspace Identity
Every workspace must define identity before features.

Required identity fields:
- Purpose
- Mission
- Primary Question
- Primary Persona
- Secondary Personas
- Expected Decisions
- Business Outcomes

Identity template:
- Purpose: Why this workspace exists in the enterprise system.
- Mission: What understanding it delivers repeatedly.
- Primary Question: The single question this workspace answers best.
- Primary Persona: The main decision owner served.
- Secondary Personas: Additional decision participants.
- Expected Decisions: Decisions this workspace enables.
- Business Outcomes: Measurable enterprise outcomes improved.

## 2. Executive Narrative
Every workspace shall follow one shared narrative sequence:

Orientation

-> Confidence

-> Decision

-> Context

-> Action

-> Continuity

-> Inquiry

Purpose of each narrative stage:
- Orientation: Establish where the executive is and what this workspace governs.
- Confidence: Show current health, stability, and trust signals.
- Decision: Surface high-consequence choices that require judgment.
- Context: Explain what changed and what constraints are active.
- Action: Present accountable next steps with expected outcomes.
- Continuity: Preserve timeline and event understanding across reviews.
- Inquiry: Allow executives to ask what to understand next.

## 3. Workspace Sections
Where appropriate, each workspace should include the following sections:
- Executive Summary
- Health / Pulse
- Decision Layer
- Context Layer
- Relationship Layer
- Recommended Actions
- Timeline
- Search / Inquiry
- Explainability

Section rationale:
- Executive Summary: Fast orientation and scope framing.
- Health / Pulse: Current enterprise condition and variance indicators.
- Decision Layer: Priority decisions sorted by consequence.
- Context Layer: Conditions, changes, and constraints influencing decisions.
- Relationship Layer: Connected enterprise effects and dependency mapping.
- Recommended Actions: Next accountable commitments.
- Timeline: Material continuity over time.
- Search / Inquiry: Directed exploration for unresolved questions.
- Explainability: Why conclusions and recommendations should be trusted.

## 4. Decision Model
Every workspace shall define:
- Top Decisions
- Decision Owners
- Business Impact
- Evidence
- Confidence
- Expected Outcome
- Risk of Inaction

Decision model contract:
- Top Decisions are ranked by enterprise consequence, not by feed recency.
- Decision Owners are explicit and accountable.
- Business Impact is stated in enterprise terms.
- Evidence is visible and traceable.
- Confidence is explicit, not implied.
- Expected Outcome is measurable and decision-linked.
- Risk of Inaction is stated clearly.

## 5. Explainability Model
Every recommendation must answer:
- Why?
- How do we know?
- What evidence supports this?
- What assumptions exist?
- How confident are we?

Explainability policy:
- Recommendations without explainability are non-conforming.
- Explainability must be understandable in executive language.
- Evidence and assumptions must be visible before commitment.

## 6. Enterprise Relationship Layer
Every workspace must connect to the Enterprise Atlas.

Required relationship fields:
- Related capabilities
- Dependencies
- Upstream impacts
- Downstream impacts
- Shared enterprise context

Relationship layer rationale:
- No workspace may operate as an isolated product surface.
- Decisions in one domain should expose impact across adjacent domains.
- Shared context preserves enterprise coherence across workspaces.

## 7. Interaction Philosophy
Core interactions:
- Hover
- Expand
- Focus
- Compare
- Filter
- Search
- Context preservation
- Navigation

Interaction rationale:
- Hover reveals immediate meaning without forcing navigation.
- Expand reveals reasoning, not noise.
- Focus reduces distraction and preserves decision context.
- Compare enables tradeoff visibility.
- Filter narrows uncertainty, not data volume alone.
- Search supports inquiry-driven understanding.
- Context preservation prevents executive disorientation.
- Navigation follows decisions, not menu density.

## 8. Experience Principles
Every workspace should feel:
- Calm
- Trustworthy
- Explainable
- Intentional
- Connected
- Premium
- Decision-centric

Experience rationale:
- Calm reduces cognitive overload.
- Trustworthy presentation improves decision confidence.
- Explainable output increases adoption.
- Intentional design reinforces executive focus.
- Connected context preserves enterprise truth.
- Premium quality communicates strategic seriousness.
- Decision-centric structure aligns software with executive purpose.

## 9. Accessibility
Every workspace blueprint implementation shall include:
- Keyboard navigation
- Reduced motion behavior
- Screen reader considerations
- Color accessibility
- Readable hierarchy

Accessibility rationale:
- Accessibility increases decision reliability under varied user conditions.
- Readable hierarchy improves comprehension speed.
- Reduced motion support protects comfort and concentration.

## 10. Workspace Success Metrics
Reusable metrics:
- Time to Understanding (TTU)
- Decision Confidence
- Decision Completion Rate
- Recommendation Acceptance
- Explainability Satisfaction
- Executive Trust

Metric definitions:
- TTU: Time from workspace entry to confident understanding.
- Decision Confidence: Self-reported confidence in decision quality.
- Decision Completion Rate: Percent of surfaced decisions that progress to clear action.
- Recommendation Acceptance: Percent of recommendations accepted with rationale.
- Explainability Satisfaction: User rating of explanation clarity and trust.
- Executive Trust: Longitudinal trust in workspace guidance.

## 11. Design Inheritance
Every future workspace shall explicitly inherit:
- Genesis Experience Manifesto
- GES-0001
- Genesis Design Philosophy
- Executive Narrative
- Genesis Product Principles
- The Genesis 30-Second Promise

Inheritance guidance:
- Genesis Experience Manifesto authoritative source: genesis/philosophy/The-Genesis-Experience-Manifesto.md
- GES-0001 authoritative source: genesis/philosophy/GES-0001/GES-0001-Genesis-Executive-Workspace-Visual-Standard.md
- Genesis Design Philosophy baseline: Design philosophy sections within Genesis Experience Manifesto and GES-0001.
- Executive Narrative baseline: Orientation -> Confidence -> Decision -> Context -> Action -> Continuity -> Inquiry.
- Genesis Product Principles baseline: product-office principles and decision discipline from product governance artifacts.
- Genesis 30-Second Promise baseline: executive should understand enterprise posture in approximately 30 seconds of orientation.

## 12. Extension Rules
Future workspaces may specialize mission and domain language, but may not fragment Genesis coherence.

Extension rules:
- Specialization is encouraged.
- Fragmentation is prohibited.
- Primary Question can change by workspace; narrative structure cannot.
- Domain-specific sections can be added; core section intent cannot be removed.
- Interaction style can adapt; uncertainty-reduction intent cannot be broken.
- Visual identity can tune accents; Genesis visual language baseline remains inherited.
- New metrics can be added; core decision confidence metrics remain mandatory.

## Workspace Lifecycle Guidance
Blueprint lifecycle for all new workspaces:
1. Identity Definition
2. Narrative Mapping
3. Section Mapping
4. Decision Model Definition
5. Explainability Validation
6. Relationship Layer Integration
7. Experience Validation
8. Freeze Candidate Review

Lifecycle checkpoint expectations:
- Checkpoint A: Primary Question and persona alignment complete.
- Checkpoint B: Narrative and section rationale complete.
- Checkpoint C: Decision and explainability models complete.
- Checkpoint D: Relationship layer and success metrics complete.
- Checkpoint E: Experience validation complete and recorded.
