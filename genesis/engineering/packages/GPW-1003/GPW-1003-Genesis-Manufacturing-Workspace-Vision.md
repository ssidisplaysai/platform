The Manufacturing Workspace is not a work-order screen.

It is not an ERP production module.

It is not a scheduling application.

Its purpose is to help leaders understand and improve enterprise production.

## Hero Question
What is preventing optimal production, and what should we do about it?

Every screen, recommendation, visualization, and interaction shall reinforce this question.

## Workspace Identity
### Purpose
Transform manufacturing from operational reporting into enterprise decision intelligence.

### Mission
Help leaders identify production constraints, quantify enterprise impact, and commit to high-value recovery actions with confidence.

### Primary Persona
Executive Leadership

### Secondary Personas
- Operations Managers
- Plant Managers
- Production Supervisors
- Manufacturing Engineers
- Quality Managers
- Supply Chain Managers
- Continuous Improvement Teams

### Expected Decisions
- Which constraints require immediate intervention
- Which production risks threaten enterprise outcomes most
- Which recovery actions unlock the greatest throughput
- Which quality degradations require escalation
- Which supplier conditions require mitigation or re-planning

### Business Outcomes
- Improved production throughput
- Reduced constraint-related revenue risk
- Better quality stability and lower defect cost
- Faster recovery from operational disruptions
- Stronger cross-functional decision alignment

## Workspace Rationale
Manufacturing is not a back-office operation.

It is a central enterprise value engine whose decisions propagate into products, customers, finance, and mission outcomes.

The workspace exists to convert manufacturing complexity into explainable strategic action.

## Executive Narrative
The Manufacturing Workspace follows the canonical narrative:

Orientation

-> Confidence

-> Decision

-> Context

-> Action

-> Continuity

-> Inquiry

Narrative purpose in Manufacturing Workspace:
- Orientation: Establish current production posture and critical operating context.
- Confidence: Surface health, reliability, and trust signals across manufacturing.
- Decision: Present highest-consequence production decisions.
- Context: Explain constraints, dependencies, and change drivers.
- Action: Recommend accountable actions with expected recovery outcomes.
- Continuity: Preserve production history, change trajectory, and decision lineage.
- Inquiry: Support focused executive investigation before commitment.

## Confidence Model
Confidence model components:
- Evidence quality
- Data freshness
- Assumption load
- Dependency stability
- Approval maturity
- Cross-capability consistency

Confidence bands:
- High: Recovery recommendation is action-ready
- Medium: Actionable with explicit caveats
- Low: Not decision-ready without additional validation

## Explainability Center
Explainability Center must answer:

- How we know

- What assumptions exist

Non-conformance rule:
Recommendations without explainability are not decision-ready.

## TTU Target
Time to Understanding target:
- Executives should understand current production posture and top constraints in under 90 seconds.
- For a focused production issue, causal context and confidence should be interpretable in under 30 seconds.

## TTD Target
Time To Decision target:
- Executives should identify the highest-value recovery decision within 3 minutes.
- High-confidence recovery recommendations should support commitment in one review cycle.
